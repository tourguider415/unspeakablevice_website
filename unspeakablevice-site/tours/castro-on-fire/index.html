Ten years after the White Night riots, the police came back. Meet the trans man doctors turned away, the lesbians who ran blood drives and wards, and the community that answered a plague and two police riots with the same refusal.

This tour includes a free round at the Lookout, overlooking the streets you’ve just walked.

Buy tickets

On this walk

  • A theater manager steps onto the marquee as police cruisers burn outside City Hall.
  • A nurse’s Polaroids appear in a pharmacy window at 18th and Castro.
  • The one bar on the street where everyone got in on a single ID.
  • An acre of pink tarp on Twin Peaks, visible for twenty miles, and why the triangle points up.

People you’ll meet

Lou Sullivan
The trans man doctors turned away for being gay, who built a community instead.
Barbara May Cameron
Hunkpapa Lakota lesbian who co-founded Gay American Indians because Castro bars wouldn’t let Native people in.
Lenore Chinn and Pat Norman
The lesbians who organized the blood drives and staffed the fight.
Bobbi Campbell, aka Sister Florence Nightmare
The KS Poster Boy, nurse, and self-declared AIDS activist before the word existed

Route & logistics

  • About 90 minutes, plus a free round of drinks afterward
  • Meets at the Castro Theatre, 429 Castro St
  • Ends at the Lookout, 3600 16th St at Market (bar is stairs-only)
  • City sidewalks with no significant hills
  • 21+ · free round, one per guest · heavy rain reschedules, tickets transfer

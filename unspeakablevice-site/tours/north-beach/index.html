See how a Latin drag queen, a Brown “bomber,” and scores of butch lesbians capitalized on the Barbary Coast to give the Victorians’ “unspeakable vice” a voice.

This tour includes a free round at a former lesbian bar in the spirit of community building in queer spaces.

Buy tickets

On this walk

  • A Spanish missionary's diary, and the two-spirit people this city's story actually starts with.
  • A Harlem Renaissance headliner in a white tuxedo, singing at 440 Broadway.
  • The WWII vet who lost a teaching career to one arrest, and won something larger at the Black Cat.
  • Two bars, one owner: the tourist show upstairs on Broadway, and the room down Adler alley where lesbians actually met.

People you’ll meet

José Sarria
Black Cat waiter, Falstaff in an apron, and in 1960 the first openly-gay candidate for public office in America.
Gladys Bentley
The Harlem Renaissance's openly queer blues star, headlining at Mona's.
Tommy Vasu
Butch entrepreneur of Tommy's Place and 12 Adler, until the police decided otherwise.

Route & logistics

  • Ends at Maggy McGary's, 1353 Grant Ave
  • Terrain is city sidewalks with one block of hillclimb
  • 21+ · free round, one per guest · heavy rain reschedules, tickets transfer

Follow a radicalizing gay liberation movement as “street queens,” hustlers, and Christian ministers form an unlikely partnership and define queer resistance.

This tour includes a free round at the Cinch Saloon (the last gay bar on Polk St.) in the spirit of community building in queer spaces.

On this walk

  • A New Year’s Day ball, a wall of police cameras, and the ministers who stepped in front of them.
  • The cafeteria where the coffee cups flew three years before Stonewall.
  • Twenty or thirty people walking the length of Polk Street. They called it Christopher Street West. You call it Pride.
  • The church that decided the neighborhood’s outcasts were its congregation.

People you’ll meet

Felicia “Flames” Elizondo
Compton’s veteran who kept the Tenderloin’s trans history alive for fifty years.
The Vanguard youth
Street kids who organized, published, and swept Market Street with brooms to make a point.
Rev. Cecil Williams and Janice Mirikitani
Social justice power couple whose radically inclusive view on faith made their ministry “the midwife of the modern LGBT movement.”
Ray Broshears
Organizer of the first Pride march, complicated on purpose.
Sylvester
Before the falsetto conquered disco, a Cockette in the Tenderloin.
Hank Wilson
Selfless hero who used his hotels to house people living with AIDS

Route & logistics

  • About 2 hours, plus a free round of drinks afterward
  • Meets at the Tenderloin Museum, 398 Eddy St
  • Ends at the Cinch Saloon, 1723 Polk St
  • Terrain is city sidewalks with no hills
  • 21+ · free round, one per guest · heavy rain reschedules, tickets transfer
